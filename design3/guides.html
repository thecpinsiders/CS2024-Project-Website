Keep your Wi-Fi up through a power cut

4 min read · Weekdays

The quickest way to lose a working day is a router that reboots every time the power flickers. RIVER 3 Plus can sit between the wall and your desk and act as a UPS (uninterruptible power supply).

Set it up in five minutes

  • Plug RIVER 3 Plus into a wall socket and leave it plugged in.
  • Plug your router, monitor and laptop charger into its AC outlets.
  • Turn on AC output. Your devices now run through it while the mains is on.
  • If the power drops, it switches to battery in under 10 ms1.

What to plug in, and what not to

Put the things that hurt most to lose first: the router and modem, then your monitor and laptop. Leave out space heaters and printers. They use a lot of power and don’t matter in a short outage.

Do it today: rate your desk to see how long it would last.

Plan power for a weekend camp

5 min read · Weekends

Plan your power like a budget. You have about 243 usable watt-hours (286 Wh × 85%). Write down what you’ll run and for how long.

An example two-night budget

  • LED lights, 15 W × 4 h × 2 nights = 120 Wh
  • Four phone charges at about 15 Wh each = 60 Wh
  • A speaker, 10 W × 5 h = 50 Wh
  • Total about 230 Wh, just inside one charge

Adding a fan or a cooler box tips it over. That’s when a solar panel or an Extra Battery earns its place.

Do it today: pick a kit that matches your budget.

Solar in real life

3 min read · Charging

RIVER 3 Plus accepts up to 220 W of solar and can fill up in about 1.5 hours1. That figure assumes ideal conditions. Here’s how to get close to it.

Three habits that matter

  • Angle the panel at the sun and re-aim it every couple of hours. Flat on the ground can cost you a lot of output.
  • Keep the station in the shade. It charges best between 0 and 45 °C1. The panel goes in the sun, the station doesn’t.
  • Avoid partial shade on the panel. One shaded strip can drag the whole panel down.

Do it today: check that the panel you’re considering stays within the 11–55 V input range1.
