Weekends that don't end at sunset.

Malaysian families pack the car for Hulu Langat, Janda Baik and Cameron Highlands to swap screens for river sounds and cool air. The hard part comes at 10 pm, when the phones are dying and the campsite's one socket has a queue.

Shop nowPlan your night

250 campsites, not all of them with a plug

Malaysia has more than 250 campsites, from riverside glamping in Selangor to highland farms in Pahang. Many don't have power hook-ups, and the ones that do are usually shared.

Source: The Straits Times, reported by OpenVan, March 2026.

What camping in Malaysia asks of you

  • Book early. Popular sites in Hulu Langat, Janda Baik and Cameron Highlands fill up on weekends, school holidays and every long weekend.
  • Ask whether your pitch has a power point. If it doesn't, River 3 Plus is your power point.
  • At riverside sites, heavy rain upstream can raise the water fast, even when it's dry where you are. Pitch well above the bank and follow the operator's warnings.

Camping in a forest reserve or state park can need a permit. Check with the site operator or the state forestry department before you go.

Road trips across the peninsula

Balik kampung for Raya, a long weekend in Cherating, the slow road up to Penang. River 3 Plus charges from the car's 12V socket while you drive, so it arrives full.

  • Charge on the highway

    About 3.3 hours from the car socket to full. That's one good stretch of the North–South Expressway.

  • Power where you stop

    A fan and lights at the beach, phones and a laptop at the R&R, and something to run when the kampung house has a power trip.

  • Fits next to the bags

    4.7 kg and about the size of a shoebox, so it doesn't take a seat.

Two nights in Cameron Highlands?

Up in the highlands the nights are cool, so you can leave the fan at home, but afternoons are often cloudy. An extra battery (572Wh in all) is a surer bet than solar. On sunny lowland trips, a foldable panel takes up to 220W.
