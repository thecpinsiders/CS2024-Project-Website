01 · Outdoors

Your first family camp in Singapore: a calm checklist

Ask parents why they haven't camped with the kids yet and the answer is rarely the outdoors. It's the fear of a hot, sleepless night and a long drive home at 2 am. This checklist is built around one goal: everyone sleeps.

One to two weeks before

  • Apply for a free NParks camping permit for a designated area, such as Areas 1 or 3 at Pasir Ris Park. You'll need a Singapore address and ID.
  • Pick a spot near the toilets. With young kids, this matters more than the view.
  • Book a barbecue pit if you want a hot dinner. Stoves and grills aren't allowed near tents.

Pack for comfort, not survival

  • A USB camping fan and a power source for it. This is the single biggest difference between a good night and a bad one.
  • An LED lantern, so nobody uses a phone torch and drains it.
  • Mosquito repellent, a groundsheet and a change of clothes for each child.
  • Something for the evening: cards, a book, or a mini projector for one film on the tent wall.

The power plan

A typical family night (fan for seven hours, lantern for four, four phone charges and one film) uses about 233 Wh. One charge of RIVER 3 Plus covers it. Staying two nights? Bring a solar panel or plan for an Extra Battery. Check your own list in the Power Planner.

The bedtime routine that works

Wash up early, while it's still light. Fan on low by 8:30 pm, lantern dimmed by 9. Keep the power station in the vestibule, in the shade and off wet ground. When the kids wake up rested, you'll be planning the next trip before breakfast.

02 · Home backup

The outage plan nobody makes, and should

Power cuts in Singapore are rare, which is exactly why most homes have no plan at all. When one happens at night, the problem isn't the dark. It's not knowing what to do first. Ten minutes now saves a stressful hour later.

1. Name the three things that must stay on

For most homes it's the Wi-Fi router, one light and phones. For some, it's a CPAP machine, a work PC in the middle of a deadline, or a baby monitor. Write them down.

2. Put those three on backup now

Plug a UPS-capable power station into the wall and plug your essentials into it. RIVER 3 Plus switches over in under 10 ms, so the router and PC never drop. If the outage runs long, save your work and shut down while there’s charge left.

3. Make the rest of the plan analogue

  • Keep a torch where everyone can find it in the dark.
  • Write down key phone numbers on paper: family, building management, your power retailer.
  • Don't use the lift during an outage. Know where the stairs are.
  • Keep the fridge closed. It stays cold for hours if you leave it alone.

4. Check it every three months

Look at the charge level in the app, test one switchover by turning off the wall socket, and make sure the cables haven't been borrowed for something else.

03 · Work anywhere

A full workday away from the wall

The dream is simple: open the laptop wherever the light is good and the noise is low. The reality is battery anxiety by 2 pm. Here is how to plan a day so that never happens.

Find shade first, view second

Laptop screens are hard to read in direct sun, and batteries of every kind prefer to stay cool. A covered pavilion or a big tree beats a perfect view.

Budget your energy like your time

Two full laptop charges (120 Wh), three camera batteries (48 Wh), four hours of a portable monitor (40 Wh) and two phone charges (24 Wh) come to 232 Wh. That's one RIVER 3 Plus charge with a little left.

Charge over USB-C when you can

Plugging the laptop into the 100W USB-C port skips the AC adapter, which wastes less energy as heat. Use the AC outlets for things that need them.

Keep it quiet on calls

At light loads RIVER 3 Plus stays under 30 dB. Put it on the ground, not the table, and your mic won't hear it.

Refill at lunch

An hour on any wall socket at a co-working space or café brings it back to full for the afternoon.
