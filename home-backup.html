When the lights go out, nothing else has to.

From November to March, the northeast monsoon brings the year's heaviest rain, especially to Kelantan, Terengganu and Pahang. A storm, a flood or a tripped breaker at 9:40 pm, and the house goes dark just when everyone's home.

Shop now

Three ways the lights go out

  • A tripped circuit at night

    The kettle and the air-con overload the circuit, and the house goes dark while you hunt for the DB box with a phone torch.

  • A planned TNB shutdown

    Maintenance and upgrade works sometimes need a planned cut in your area. You get a notice, but your Wi-Fi doesn't read it.

  • Monsoon storms

    Lightning, fallen trees and rising water can cut supply for hours, and in a flood the power is often switched off for safety. Keep River charged before the rainy season starts.

Set it up once, then forget it's there

Plug River 3 Plus into the wall, and plug your router, desktop or fish-tank pump into River. If the power drops, it switches to battery in under 10 ms. The EcoFlow app shows how much charge is left.

Medical equipment at home

If someone sleeps with a CPAP, a power cut at night matters more. Here's an honest guide to what River 3 Plus can and can't keep running.

Medical equipment on one charge
EquipmentTypical drawOn one charge
CPAP, humidifier off10–25 WAbout 10–24 hours
CPAP, humidifier onAbout 53 WAbout 4.5 hours
Home oxygen concentrator250–350 WUnder 1 hour

A CPAP machine can get through a night-time power cut, especially with the humidifier off. A home oxygen concentrator draws too much for this size, so River 3 Plus isn't a backup plan for one. Figures are typical: ResMed lists the AirSense 10 at about 53 W with its humidifier, and most 5-litre oxygen concentrators draw 250–350 W. Always check your device's label, and talk to your doctor or equipment provider before relying on any backup.
